One satisfying probability can reshape everything you don't know.

Most NLP in finance asks: "Is this positive or negative?" That's only half the picture. It captures how people FEEL — not what they believe EXISTS.

I've been building something called Demand-Side Semantic Scoring. It separates existence from sentiment in news text, converts both into ordered probability distributions, and then does something interesting:

You supply ONE known probability — say p(sanctions) = 0.80 from a prediction market — and it ripples through the entire ordered set.

Here's what happens mechanically:

The system decomposes a news event into domain-critical concepts (tariff, risk, contagion, recession, volatility...), orders them by frequency and proximity, then assigns a linear prior. That prior is naive. But the moment you anchor ONE concept to a known probability, the ordering enforces constraints on everything else.

Concepts ranked below the anchor get compressed into (0, anchor_p).
Concepts ranked above get stretched into (anchor_p, 1).
Each concept gets a unique probability determined by its position relative to the anchor.

One number in. An entire calibrated risk distribution out.

That's the ripple effect. It's not sentiment. It's not topic modeling. It's converting the logical structure of language into probability space — then using a single empirical datapoint as a fulcrum to reshape the whole distribution.

The implications: you can take any qualitative intelligence report, any earnings call transcript, any geopolitical briefing — decompose it into semantic primitives, anchor it to one thing you actually know, and derive calibrated probabilities for everything else in the narrative.

Geopolitical risk. Supply chain disruption. Macro regime change. All quantifiable from text + one anchor.
